# Institutional Evidence Editorial

Aliases: university leadership, accreditation briefing, evidence-first executive deck

## Purpose

Use for dean, provost, accreditation, accessibility, institutional strategy, and higher-education investor presentations where trust, operational evidence, and human consequence matter more than visual spectacle.

## Style Lock

### Typography System

Use a restrained humanist sans-serif with strong sentence-case headlines, compact evidence labels, and readable source notes. Maintain clear scale separation; avoid oversized marketing typography on working slides.

### Layout Grid And Spacing

Use a disciplined editorial grid with generous outer margins, straight alignment, thin rules, and one dominant visual argument. Alternate image-led, evidence-led, and decision-led compositions to create pacing.

### Component Grammar

Use hard-edged image crops, square or lightly rounded evidence frames, thin dividers, direct labels, and small numbered markers. Avoid nested cards, decorative pills, heavy shadows, and floating UI chrome.

### Semantic Colors And Ratios

Favor warm gray or off-white canvas, near-black text, one deep institutional accent, one restrained systems accent, and burgundy only for material risk. Preserve approved university or product brand colors as locked tokens.

### Photography And Evidence

Prefer documentary-feeling institutional environments, real work artifacts, product-in-context, and anonymous professional workflows. GPT Image 2 scenes must feel specific to the claimed work and carry the required visible generated-scene disclosure.

### Data And Diagram Behavior

Use direct labels, restrained charts, and physical evidence before abstract diagrams. Keep metrics and citations native and editable. Use diagrams only when the relationship itself is the proof.

### Header, Footer, Sources, And Logos

Use quiet section markers and readable source notes. Add logos as approved native assets with consistent clear space; never ask GPT Image 2 to recreate them.

### Density Limits

One leadership conclusion per slide. Normal slides use one headline, one proof line, and up to three evidence groups. Leave-behind slides may carry more detail when explicitly designed for reading.

### GPT Image 2 Prompt Prefix

`model: gpt-image-2. Create a realistic institutional editorial scene with natural materials, credible professional behavior, restrained saturation, composed contrast, and clear negative space for native PowerPoint text.`

### Negative Constraints

No generic AI circuitry, glowing brains, holograms, futuristic dashboards, fake university marks, stock-photo handshakes, all-navy monotony, decorative gradients, illegible document text, model-rendered logos, or invented institutional claims.

### QA Checks

Verify leadership relevance, claim-image alignment, credible human behavior, style isolation, presentation-distance readability, native editability of critical information, brand-asset fidelity, and visible GPT Image 2 disclosure.
